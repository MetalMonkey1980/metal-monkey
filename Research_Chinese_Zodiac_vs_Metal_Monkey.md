# Research — Chinese Zodiac vs. Metal Monkey Perspective

**Project:** Metal Monkey  
**Document type:** Research / Working Document  
**Status:** Exploratory  
**Purpose:** Compare traditional Chinese Zodiac descriptions with the Metal Monkey model and identify possible patterns for further research.

---

## 1. Why this research exists

The Metal Monkey project does not attempt to prove that the Chinese Zodiac is scientifically correct.

Instead, we use the Chinese Zodiac as an existing long-term cultural model of human differences and examine whether some of its recurring patterns can be interpreted through another framework.

Our starting question is:

> **What if the animals of the Chinese Zodiac are not labels describing what a person is, but symbols describing different ways in which people may naturally perceive, function and interact with others?**

This distinction is fundamental.

The purpose of this research is therefore not to classify people.

It is to investigate whether the traditional system contains patterns that can be reinterpreted as:

- different **Lenses** — how a person perceives the world,
- different **Trines** — how a person tends to function,
- different **Temptations** — how a person may naturally position themselves within a social system,
- and different forms of **harmony, complementarity and conflict** between people.

The results remain hypotheses until independently tested.

---

# 2. What we found in the traditional Zodiac

The twelve animals are traditionally grouped into four Three Harmonies (*San He*):

| Trine | Animals |
|---|---|
| 1 | Rat – Dragon – Monkey |
| 2 | Ox – Snake – Rooster |
| 3 | Tiger – Horse – Dog |
| 4 | Rabbit – Goat – Pig |

The traditional descriptions of these groups and individual animals vary considerably between sources and traditions.

They should therefore be treated as **source material**, not as fixed scientific definitions.

---

# 3. Our first discovery — the Trines may describe different ways of functioning

The original Metal Monkey project already used the Trines as a layer describing **how a person functions**.

During research we noticed something interesting.

The animals inside one Trine do not necessarily behave identically.

Instead, they may express a common underlying theme in different ways.

This led us to ask:

> **Could the three animals within each Trine represent three different social approaches to the same general domain?**

This question led to the next hypothesis.

---

# 4. The hypothesis of the three Temptations

We originally described three possible social positions:

1. **To Lead**
2. **To Explore**
3. **To Observe**

We deliberately use the word **Temptation** because this is not intended to mean a person's destiny or fixed role.

It describes something a person may naturally be **drawn toward** when interacting with other people.

A person can learn to perform another role.

A person can also reject their natural tendency.

Therefore:

> **Temptation is not identity. It is attraction.**

---

# 5. Trine 1 — Rat, Dragon, Monkey

## Traditional starting point

The Rat–Dragon–Monkey Trine is traditionally associated with intelligence, adaptability, ambition, resourcefulness and movement.

### Dragon — Lead

The Dragon is traditionally associated with power, ambition, authority and strong presence.

Our interpretation:

> **Dragon leads through vision and authority.**

Possible internal logic:

> “I see where we should go.”

This is different from simply being dominant.

### Monkey — Explore

The Monkey is traditionally associated with curiosity, cleverness, adaptability and ingenuity.

Our interpretation:

> **Monkey explores through experimentation.**

Possible internal logic:

> “What happens if I try this?”

This is why we distinguish **exploration** from invention.

An explorer does not necessarily invent something new.

They may simply be curious enough to discover what happens.

### Rat — Observe

The Rat is traditionally associated with intelligence, resourcefulness and adaptability.

Our interpretation:

> **Rat observes in order to understand the situation and adapt.**

Possible internal logic:

> “Let me see what is happening before I decide what to do.”

### Working result

**Dragon → Lead**  
**Monkey → Explore**  
**Rat → Observe**

This was our first strong indication that the three roles might exist across different Trines.

---

# 6. Trine 2 — Ox, Snake, Rooster

## Traditional starting point

The Ox–Snake–Rooster Trine is commonly associated with persistence, discipline, planning, precision and practical achievement.

### Ox — Lead

Ox is traditionally associated with strength, persistence, patience and endurance.

Our interpretation:

> **Ox leads by maintaining direction and carrying the group forward through persistence.**

Possible internal logic:

> “We have chosen the direction. I will make sure we stay with it.”

This differs significantly from Dragon leadership.

**Dragon:** “I see the direction.”

**Ox:** “I will hold the direction.”

### Snake — Observe

Snake is traditionally associated with intelligence, calculation, observation and strategy.

Our interpretation:

> **Snake observes in order to understand and plan.**

Possible internal logic:

> “I will watch, understand the situation and determine what should happen next.”

This differs from Rat:

**Rat:** observes to adapt.

**Snake:** observes to plan.

### Rooster — Explore? **OPEN QUESTION**

This is one of the most important unresolved questions.

Rooster is traditionally associated with precision, confidence, organization, decisiveness and attention to detail.

Initially this did not appear to fit the word *Explorer*.

However, we realized that exploration does not necessarily mean travelling into unknown territory.

It may also mean:

> **finding a better solution to an existing problem.**

This creates a possible interpretation:

> **Rooster explores by identifying imperfections and searching for a better solution.**

Possible internal logic:

> “This could work better. There must be a better way.”

This remains an **open hypothesis**.

---

# 7. Trine 3 — Tiger, Horse, Dog

## Traditional starting point

Tiger–Horse–Dog are commonly associated with independence, courage, action, loyalty and strong ideals.

### Tiger — Lead

Tiger is frequently described as courageous, decisive, independent and authoritative.

Our interpretation:

> **Tiger leads by going first.**

Possible internal logic:

> “Someone has to go first. I'll go.”

This differs from:

- Dragon — vision
- Ox — endurance
- Tiger — personal example / initiative

### Horse — Explore

Horse is associated with freedom, movement, independence and dislike of restriction.

Our interpretation:

> **Horse explores by moving toward what lies beyond the current boundary.**

Possible internal logic:

> “What is beyond the horizon?”

This is a different form of exploration from Monkey.

**Monkey explores possibilities.**

**Horse explores space and freedom.**

### Dog — Observe

Dog is traditionally associated with loyalty, vigilance, responsibility, protection and justice.

Our interpretation:

> **Dog observes in order to protect the group and maintain what it considers right.**

Possible internal logic:

> “Is everyone safe? Is something wrong? Does this direction violate what we believe?”

This differs from:

- Rat — observation for adaptation
- Snake — observation for strategy
- Dog — observation for protection and principles

### Working result

**Tiger → Lead**  
**Horse → Explore**  
**Dog → Observe**

---

# 8. Trine 4 — Rabbit, Goat, Pig

This Trine was especially important because it gave us an opportunity to test whether our hypothesis could survive a completely different thematic environment.

Rabbit–Goat–Pig are traditionally associated with harmony, sensitivity, creativity, care and cooperation.

Our question became:

> **Can we find a Leader, Explorer and Observer even within a Trine whose common theme is harmony and relationships?**

### Rabbit — Observe

Rabbit is commonly associated with diplomacy, sensitivity, caution and awareness of social atmosphere.

Our interpretation:

> **Rabbit observes harmony.**

Possible internal logic:

> “What is happening between these people, and where is the tension?”

This creates a new form of observation.

### Goat — Explore

Goat is traditionally associated with creativity, aesthetics, sensitivity and appreciation of beauty.

Our interpretation:

> **Goat explores by discovering new possibilities for harmony, beauty and balance.**

This led to an important refinement:

> **An Explorer does not have to invent something.**

A person may discover something that already exists and recognize:

> “This could work beautifully here.”

This is a fundamentally different kind of exploration from Monkey or Horse.

### Pig — Lead

Pig is traditionally associated with generosity, openness, warmth and cooperation.

Our working interpretation:

> **Pig may lead by creating unity.**

Possible internal logic:

> “Let us find a way for everyone to belong.”

This suggests a fourth form of leadership:

- Dragon — leads through vision
- Ox — leads through endurance
- Tiger — leads through personal action
- Pig — may lead through unity

This remains a hypothesis requiring further research.

---

# 9. Working Matrix

Our current interpretation therefore looks like this:

| Social Temptation | Trine 1 | Trine 2 | Trine 3 | Trine 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Lead** | Dragon — vision | Ox — endurance | Tiger — first mover | Pig — unity? |
| **Explore** | Monkey — experiment | Rooster — better solution? | Horse — new space | Goat — new harmony |
| **Observe** | Rat — adapt | Snake — plan | Dog — protect | Rabbit — harmony |

This is **not a conclusion**.

It is our current research model.

The question is not:

> “Can we make all twelve animals fit?”

The question is:

> **“Does the model continue to explain observations without us forcing the observations into the model?”**

---

# 10. A second discovery — different kinds of the same role

This may be one of the most important findings so far.

The word **Leader** is not necessarily one personality type.

We may have:

- **Dragon:** leader because it sees the direction.
- **Ox:** leader because it can maintain the direction.
- **Tiger:** leader because it is willing to go first.
- **Pig:** leader because it can unite people.

Likewise, **Observer** may have different purposes:

- Rat observes → **adapt**
- Snake observes → **plan**
- Dog observes → **protect**
- Rabbit observes → **maintain harmony**

And **Explorer** may also have different forms:

- Monkey explores → **experiment**
- Horse explores → **move beyond boundaries**
- Goat explores → **discover harmonious possibilities**
- Rooster explores? → **find a better solution**

This distinction may be more important than the animal itself.

---

# 11. Traditional relationships between animals

The traditional Zodiac also contains relationships that cross the Trines.

Four Trines are not the only relationships in the system.

There are also traditional harmonious pairings and opposing/clashing relationships.

These relationships are relevant to our research because they may provide another opportunity to examine whether different ways of functioning can create either:

- conflict,
- complementarity,
- or a bridge.

A traditional clash should not automatically be interpreted as hatred or inevitable failure.

For Metal Monkey, a clash is more interesting when understood as:

> **two different ways of approaching the same situation.**

---

# 12. Why the relationships became important to Metal Monkey

We noticed that some traditional animal relationships can be interpreted through our proposed social roles.

For example:

### Dragon × Dog

Our hypothesis:

**Dragon → Lead through vision**

**Dog → Observe to protect**

A possible conflict:

> Dragon: “I see where we need to go.”

> Dog: “I need to know whether that direction is right for the group.”

The disagreement is not necessarily about good versus bad.

It may be a disagreement about **what responsible leadership means**.

---

### Tiger × Monkey

Our hypothesis:

**Tiger → Lead through action**

**Monkey → Explore through experimentation**

Possible tension:

> Tiger: “Let's act.”

> Monkey: “Wait. What if we try something else first?”

Again, neither approach is automatically wrong.

---

### Dragon × Rooster

If our hypothesis about Rooster proves correct:

**Dragon → vision**

**Rooster → better implementation / solution**

then the relationship becomes potentially complementary:

> One provides direction.  
> The other improves the way that direction is executed.

This is exactly the kind of **bridge** we want Metal Monkey to investigate.

---

# 13. A possible new principle

Our research has therefore produced a possible principle:

> **People may share the same social temptation while pursuing it through completely different methods.**

For example:

**Three Leaders do not necessarily want the same kind of leadership.**

They may disagree precisely because all three believe they understand what leadership should be.

Similarly:

**Three Explorers may disagree about what it means to explore correctly.**

And:

**Three Observers may disagree about what deserves to be observed.**

This could explain why people with apparently similar roles can still experience conflict.

But it can also create a bridge.

---

# 14. Personal observations as research input

During the development of Metal Monkey, several real people appeared to resemble some of the proposed patterns.

For example:

- A person born under Pig was observed to naturally take leadership through charisma, personality and the ability to seek unity.
- A person associated with Goat appeared to naturally notice new trends and possibilities that were harmonious with their values and then make use of them.

These observations are useful because they generate hypotheses.

They are not sufficient to establish a general rule.

The correct next step is therefore:

> **Observe more people. Compare them. Record counterexamples. Look for patterns without selecting only confirming cases.**

---

# 15. What we must NOT conclude

This research does **not** demonstrate that:

- every person born in a particular year behaves according to the corresponding animal;
- the Chinese Zodiac scientifically determines personality;
- the traditional descriptions are scientifically validated;
- a person can be accurately judged from their birth year alone;
- a traditional compatibility relationship predicts whether two people will succeed together.

The Chinese Zodiac is being used here as a **source of patterns and hypotheses**.

The Metal Monkey interpretation is our own conceptual model.

---

# 16. The Elephant Principle

This research must follow the same principle as the rest of Metal Monkey:

> **Do not force the elephant to fit the map.**

If observations contradict the model, the model must change.

If a new observation creates a better explanation, the model should evolve.

The purpose of the research is not to prove Metal Monkey right.

The purpose is to discover **whether there is something worth understanding.**

---

# 17. Current Open Questions

1. Is **Rooster → Explore** a valid interpretation?
2. Is **Pig → Lead through unity** supported by broader observation?
3. Can the three Temptations be identified consistently across all four Trines?
4. Are the different forms of Leading, Exploring and Observing actually useful categories?
5. Can traditional harmony relationships be interpreted as **bridges between different ways of functioning**?
6. Can traditional clashes be interpreted as **different perspectives competing over the same social role**?
7. Can we find examples that contradict our current classification?
8. Can the model eventually be tested without revealing the expected answer to participants?

---

# 18. Current conclusion

At this stage, we have not discovered a truth.

We have discovered a **pattern worth investigating**.

The most interesting observation is not:

> “Dragon is a leader.”

It is:

> **“Dragon, Ox, Tiger and possibly Pig may all be capable of leadership, but their reasons for leading and their methods of leadership may be fundamentally different.”**

The same may apply to Exploring and Observing.

If this survives further research, the Chinese Zodiac may provide an unexpectedly useful starting point for illustrating a central Metal Monkey idea:

> **People may be doing similar things for completely different reasons — and different people may be doing completely different things while fulfilling the same underlying role.**

That difference can create conflict.

But it can also create a bridge.

---

## Research discipline

This document is intentionally exploratory.

Interpretations must not be adjusted merely to make the model symmetrical.

When a traditional characteristic does not fit the proposed Metal Monkey structure, the mismatch must be recorded rather than hidden.

Future research should include:

- supporting examples,
- counterexamples,
- alternative interpretations,
- source comparison,
- and, where possible, structured testing.

The goal is not confirmation.

The goal is understanding.
