## Why Metal Monkey Exists

Metal Monkey began with a simple observation:

People are different.

They perceive the same situation differently, react differently, value different things, and can be naturally attracted to or repelled by one another.

These differences are often interpreted as personal flaws.

One person is called chaotic.
Another is called rigid.
One is considered too emotional.
Another too cold.
One is seen as passive.
Another as controlling.

But what if these differences are not necessarily defects?

What if they are different ways of perceiving and interacting with the same world?

Metal Monkey was created to explore this question.

---

## The Starting Point

The Chinese Zodiac provided the original cultural framework that led to these observations.

However, Metal Monkey is not an attempt to prove or preserve the Chinese Zodiac as a fixed system of personality classification.

It is a starting map.

The project takes observations traditionally associated with the Chinese Zodiac and attempts to translate them into a more general language that can be understood independently of Chinese culture.

The purpose is not to tell people what they are.

The purpose is to explore why people may behave differently, why some combinations naturally create cooperation, why others create conflict, and whether these differences can be described through a meaningful structure.

---

## Observation Comes Before Explanation

Metal Monkey did not begin with a finished theory.

It developed from observation.

Different people.
Different cultures.
Different environments.
Different conflicts.
Different ways of solving problems.

Over time, recurring patterns appeared.

The project attempts to describe these patterns and determine whether they can be organized into a coherent model.

The model is therefore allowed to evolve.

Existing definitions are working definitions, not immutable laws.

If new observations reveal that an existing definition is incomplete, inaccurate, or unnecessarily complicated, the definition should be reconsidered.

The project must follow the observations, not force the observations to follow the model.

---

## The Model Is Not the Truth

Metal Monkey does not claim that its current model represents an objective or scientifically established truth about human personality.

It is an experimental framework.

A person may recognize themselves in a particular description.

That recognition is interesting.

It is not, by itself, proof.

The purpose of the project is therefore not to create another system in which people can be placed into boxes.

It is to create a structure that can be questioned, tested, improved, challenged, or ultimately rejected.

If the model fails, the model must change.

If parts of the model repeatedly correspond with observations, those parts deserve further investigation.

---

## From Cultural Symbols to General Concepts

The project gradually separates the original cultural symbols from the concepts they may represent.

The animals are not intended to define a person's identity.

The elements are not intended to determine a person's destiny.

Instead, these symbols are used as a language for exploring different dimensions of human behaviour and perception.

The emerging model currently explores several layers:

**Lens** — how a person naturally perceives the world.

**Trigon** — how a person tends to function and direct their energy.

**Intersection / Temptation** — a possible layer describing the roles or behavioural directions a person may naturally be drawn toward.

**Yin / Yang** — a possible polarity describing the direction in which energy is expressed.

These definitions remain open to further observation and development.

---

## Curiosity Instead of Classification

The central purpose of Metal Monkey is not classification.

It is curiosity.

Instead of asking:

> "What type of person is this?"

we want to encourage questions such as:

> "Why does this person see the situation differently?"

> "What am I not seeing from my own position?"

> "Why do I admire this person?"

> "Why does this person irritate me?"

> "What could I learn from someone whose natural way of thinking is different from mine?"

The model should become an invitation to learn rather than a reason to judge.

---

## The Research Attitude

Metal Monkey follows a simple principle:

**Observe.  
Listen.  
Look for patterns.  
Form hypotheses.  
Test them.  
Look for exceptions.  
Improve the model.**

Confirmation alone is not enough.

A useful model must also survive encounters with observations that do not fit it.

The project therefore welcomes disagreement, counterexamples, and unexpected results.

A contradiction is not necessarily a failure of the project.

It may be the most useful information the project can receive.

---

## The Larger Question

The deeper question behind Metal Monkey is not:

> "Is the Chinese Zodiac correct?"

The deeper question is:

> **"Are there underlying patterns in the way people perceive, function, interact, and conflict that we have not yet learned to describe clearly?"**

The Chinese Zodiac is one possible map.

Metal Monkey is an attempt to explore the landscape beyond the map.

Perhaps the model will eventually explain something meaningful.

Perhaps parts of it will need to be discarded.

Perhaps the entire model will lead somewhere unexpected.

The project is open to all three possibilities.

---

## The Elephant

Every person sees the world from a particular position.

Every position reveals something.

Every position also hides something.

The elephant is larger than the part any one of us can see.

Understanding another person does not require abandoning our own perspective.

It requires becoming aware that it is only one perspective among many.

That is why the project begins with curiosity rather than certainty.

And that is why its guiding principle remains:

**Keep looking.  
Keep listening.  
The elephant is bigger than any of us.**
